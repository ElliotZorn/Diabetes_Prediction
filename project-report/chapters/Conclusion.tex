\section{Conclusion}

These findings do not have great utility in the area of diabetes prediction, as models have been trained on this dataset, particularly that in Ayon et al. (2019), that result in much higher accuracy (98.35\%) than what was achieved in this project (79.22\%).
A model with high accuracy would be particularly beneficial for doctors wishing to expand their ability to diagnose diabetes, or doctors trying to diagnose diabetes in environments where testing resources are scarce or expensive.
Because the predictor variables are relatively easy to collect for almost any female patient, the model could be run for a far greater number of patients than tests.
The model could then be run for all patients, or patients meeting a less stringent diagnostic criteria than what may be required for a test, and tests could then be given to patients predicted to have diabetes by the model in addition to those fitting traditional diagnostic criteria.
However, as stated, this model does not reach a high enough accuracy standard for those options to be viable.
