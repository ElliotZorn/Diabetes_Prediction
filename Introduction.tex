\chapter{Introduction}

Diabetes is a chronic autoimmune disease characterized by the loss of function in the pancreatic beta cells, which aid the body in producing insulin. Insulin is a crucial hormone that regulates blood glucose levels. When the cells are damaged or destroyed, the body is no longer able to regulate glucose effectively leading to the patient going into diabetic ketoacidosis.  It should be noted that there are several forms of diabetes: Type 1, Type 2, and Gestational diabetes. While Type 1 diabetes is more commonly diagnosed in juvenile patients and involves the complete destruction of beta cells, Type 2 develops more gradually in the patient and is seen more in adults. As the early symptoms of diabetes are commonly overlooked as they are subtle and overlap with other less severe health problems, the disease can go undiagnosed and untreated for a long time. This increases the risk of long-term complications like cardiovascular disease, kidney failure, loss of eyesight, and even death. 

Because an early diagnosis and intervention can be critical in the healthcare system, a model that could diagnose diabetes would be very insightful. The use of machine learning to aid early detection and prediction of diabetes has received increased attention in the past decade. Machine learning models can uncover patterns in the clinical data that may not inherently be obvious to the human doctor. This helps the healthcare professional identify more at-risk patients with greater accuracy and speed. 

In this paper, a multilayer perceptron (MLP) model is proposed to predict the presence of diabetes using data from the Pima Indian Diabetes Database, which was compiled by the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases. This dataset includes various medical and personal attributes of female patients of Pima Indian heritage, such as glucose levels, body mass index (MBI), age, and number of pregnancies. The MLP model is developed and then refined by tuning its hyperparameters to improve the accuracy of the predictive performance. The goal is to demonstrate the potential of neural networks in producing early detection and diagnosis of diabetes through the dataset.
